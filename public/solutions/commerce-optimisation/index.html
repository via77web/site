SOLUTION / Commerce Optimisation

Your store should perform better six months after launch than it did on day one.

Customer behaviour provides information no design workshop can fully predict. Commerce Optimisation turns that behaviour into a structured programme of improvements.

What does commerce optimisation involve?

Commerce optimisation is a continuing cycle of observation, research, prioritisation, experimentation and implementation. It examines where customers struggle, tests changes against agreed measures and uses the findings to guide the next decision. Work can cover navigation, search, product pages, cart, checkout, mobile usability, speed and retention. The appropriate method depends on traffic, data quality and the commercial problem.

01

Observe and analyse

Combine analytics, customer feedback and behaviour research. Check measurement quality before interpreting a funnel.

02

Prioritise

Compare likely impact, evidence, effort and operational dependencies. Keep the commercial question explicit.

03

Experiment

Define the hypothesis, primary measure and guardrails. Use A/B testing when traffic supports it; use research when it does not.

04

Implement

Improve PDPs, navigation, search, cart, checkout, landing pages, site speed, merchandising, mobile UX, forms and content hierarchy.

05

Measure and repeat

Assess the result, record what was learned and update the development roadmap. Include retention, upsell and cross-sell where relevant.

SCOPE / OWNERSHIP / NEXT STEPS

When the funnel shows a problem but not its cause

For trading teams with unanswered questions about discovery, product choice or checkout. Check event accuracy and segment differences before interpreting a headline conversion rate.

Concrete deliverables

A measurement review, prioritised hypothesis backlog, experiment or research plan, implementation scope and a decision record describing results and uncertainty.

Who does what

Your team provides analytics access, trading context and campaign dates. VIA77 defines the method; both teams agree the primary measure and commercial guardrails before a change ships.

Investment, timing and support

Traffic, seasonality, purchase frequency and measurement quality govern timing. Lower-traffic stores may need task research instead of an underpowered A/B test. Ongoing scope follows the evidence.

A useful question: Can you guarantee a conversion uplift?

No. An experiment can be inconclusive or negative. The useful outcome is a defensible decision, including reverting a change when the evidence does not support it.
