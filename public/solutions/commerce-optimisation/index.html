SOLUTION / ONGOING IMPROVEMENT

Keep improving your Shopify store after launch.

Most stores do not need another redesign. They need someone to fix what matters, ship steadily and keep a clear list of what comes next.

What does ongoing Shopify improvement look like?

An agreed backlog of fixes, features and experiments, worked through in priority order, with review points to check what changed. It combines development with design and analysis, so requests get judged against evidence rather than whoever asked last.

Who it suits

  • Live Shopify stores with no dedicated developer.
  • Internal teams that need specialist support.
  • Brands that have just launched and have a list of next steps.
  • Merchants with a theme that has gathered too many quick fixes.

When it becomes relevant

  • The to-do list keeps growing but little ships.
  • Small changes break other things.
  • Apps have piled up and pages are getting slower.
  • You want to act on data but have no one to do it.

What is included

  • A store review and starting backlog.
  • Prioritisation based on evidence, effort and business need.
  • Agreed design and development work.
  • Release checks and a change record.
  • Periodic reviews of results and priorities.

What depends on scope

  • The amount and mix of work.
  • Whether analytics or research is included.
  • Support expectations, which are agreed in writing.
  • Custom features that need their own scope.
01

Review

We look at the store, data, apps and existing request list.

02

Prioritise

We rank the work together and agree what comes first.

03

Ship

Changes get built, tested and released with a record of what changed.

04

Review results

We check what happened, allowing for promotions and seasonality, then update the backlog.

Who does what

You provide

  • Store and analytics access.
  • Business context, such as campaigns and stock.
  • A person who can approve work.

VIA77 owns

  • Agreed changes and their quality.
  • The backlog and change record.
  • Honest reporting.

Platforms and third parties

  • Shopify changes its platform on its own schedule.
  • App vendors own their updates and support.
  • Your team owns campaigns and daily trading unless agreed otherwise.

What affects cost and timing

We scope and quote after discovery rather than publishing a fixed price, because these factors move the work most:

  • Volume and complexity of work.
  • State of the current theme.
  • Tracking quality.
  • Speed of approvals.

After launch

This solution is the after-launch stage. It should be easy to pause or stop, and your store, code and documentation should stay usable if you do.
