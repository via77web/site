SOLUTION / MIGRATION AND REPLATFORMING

Moving to Shopify, or rebuilding the store you have.

A migration is a business change with a website attached. We plan what moves, what changes and what cannot transfer, so risks are known before the switch rather than discovered after it.

What does a Shopify migration involve?

It covers moving catalogue and content, mapping old URLs to new ones, carrying over search metadata, deciding what customer and order history is needed, reconnecting integrations, then rehearsing before launch. Search visibility can still move during any migration. A good plan reduces the risk but cannot promise zero ranking loss.

Who it suits

  • Brands on Magento, WooCommerce or a custom platform considering Shopify.
  • Shopify merchants whose theme needs a rebuild.
  • Teams with costly maintenance or fragile releases.
  • Businesses merging several stores or brands.

When it becomes relevant

  • Every small change requires a developer and a risky release.
  • Hosting, security and extension upkeep take too much time and budget.
  • The current platform limits how you sell.
  • The store has good search traffic that must be protected during a rebuild.

What moves, and what needs a decision

AreaWhat to plan
CatalogueMap categories, attributes and variants. Complex configurable products usually need manual review.
ContentDecide which pages and articles to keep, merge or retire.
URLs and redirectsInventory valuable URLs and map each to the closest useful page. Avoid chains.
SEO metadataCarry over titles, descriptions, headings and structured data where they still fit.
CustomersRecords can import. Passwords cannot, and marketing consent should be mapped carefully.
OrdersDecide what history is needed and how it will be imported, then do it after products and customers.
IntegrationsList what each connection does today and how it will work after launch.
Launch checksTest orders, emails, redirects, analytics, domains and sender authentication.

What depends on scope

  • Catalogue size and complexity.
  • Amount of historical data worth keeping.
  • Custom functionality that needs replacing.
  • Number of integrations, stores and languages.
  • Whether the design also changes.

What affects cost and timing

We scope and quote after discovery rather than publishing a fixed price, because these factors move the work most:

  • Catalogue and data complexity.

After launch

Plan to monitor closely after launch. Check for 404 errors, redirect gaps, order and payment issues, integration failures and changes in search coverage. Compare results against your baseline while allowing for seasonality, promotions and stock changes.

QUESTIONS BUYERS ASK

Before you commit.

Will we lose rankings when we migrate?＋

Rankings can move during any migration and no one should promise otherwise. Good redirects, preserved content and careful checks reduce the risk.

Can customer passwords be migrated?＋

No. Shopify says passwords cannot be imported, so customers need to activate accounts or set a new password. That needs a communication plan.

Can we bring across order history?＋

Often, yes, but not through Shopify’s customer CSV import. It normally needs an app or API method, so decide how far back you really need.

Do Shopify redirects work on any old URL?＋

Shopify redirects apply only when the old path no longer loads a page, and some paths are reserved. Test the redirect map on the real store before launch.
