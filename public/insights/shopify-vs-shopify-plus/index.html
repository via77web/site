Shopify vs Shopify Plus: when does upgrading make sense?

Assess a Plus upgrade against your requirements and operating costs, not your revenue or the plan name.

Published by VIA77. Last reviewed 5 November 2026.

The short answer

Upgrade when a specific requirement cannot be met on your current plan and solving it is worth the higher ongoing cost. Plus currently is currently listed at A$4,000 a month on a one-year term or A$3,700 on a three-year term, and higher-volume businesses may pay a variable fee. Many stores can solve slow pages or messy navigation without changing plan.

Start with the requirement

An upgrade makes sense when a documented business requirement cannot be met appropriately by the current setup and the value of resolving that constraint justifies the ongoing cost. Revenue alone is not a complete decision rule. Write down the workflow that is failing, the commercial effect and the capability a different plan must provide.

Separate a platform limit from an implementation problem

Slow templates, inconsistent product data and unclear navigation are not automatically reasons to change plans. First establish whether the constraint comes from the subscription, an app, an integration or the way the storefront was built. A targeted improvement may remove the problem without a broader change.

Build a requirements matrix

For each requirement, record the customer or operational task, the current limitation, the proposed solution and the person responsible for validating it. Examples include wholesale purchasing rules, checkout requirements, multiple operating teams and integrations. Check each requirement against Shopify’s current plan documentation and the actual account configuration.

Compare the full operating cost

Include the subscription, app changes, integration maintenance, implementation work and the time your team needs to operate the new setup. Estimate the cost of keeping the current system as well. Use a range when the evidence is uncertain rather than presenting a precise return on investment that the data cannot support.

Validate before committing

Use a representative workflow to test the recommendation. Ask whether it handles real catalogue complexity, exceptions and ownership after launch. Confirm current pricing and contractual details directly with Shopify before making a commitment.

A useful decision

The output should be a documented recommendation: stay and improve, upgrade for an identified requirement, or investigate further. Each option needs assumptions, dependencies and a named next action. VIA77 can assess that decision alongside the broader commerce architecture.
